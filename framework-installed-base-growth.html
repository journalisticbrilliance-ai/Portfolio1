Growth Operating Model

Installed-Base Growth Operating Model

How one customer success manager grows a medical equipment company's existing lab accounts across three states without hurting uptime. Built for a company that sells to Mohs surgery labs.

Fix first, then grow
Proposed model · built from public information · lab data is illustrative
⭐
Purpose & positioning
Grow revenue inside labs the company already serves, and keep every lab running while doing it.
The rule
A Mohs lab runs on same-day surgery. If the cryostat is down or the blades run out, patients wait. So health comes first. Uptime earns the trust that expansion needs.
What it is
  • ›A model for one CSM covering Florida, Georgia, and Tennessee
  • ›Focused on growing existing accounts
  • ›Runs on service data and sales data joined on one account ID
  • ›Health decides what gets sold
What makes it different
  • ›Uptime comes before any pitch
  • ›Every play starts from evidence, not quota
  • ›CSM sells small items, KAM sells capital
  • ›Roles and credit are settled before launch
🔁
The process
Six steps, run on repeat for every account.
Step 1
Map accounts
Match sales and service records on one account ID. Pull current spend per lab.
Step 2
Score health
Green, yellow, or red from downtime, repeat tickets, reorders, and contract date.
Step 3
Find the gaps
White space map: what each lab buys from us, buys elsewhere, or doesn't have.
Step 4
Rank and plan
Gap size, health, and likelihood to buy. One-page plan for top accounts.
Step 5
Run the play
Consumables, then service plans, then training, then capital.
Step 6
Hand off and track
CSM logs the opportunity. KAM accepts or declines. Reviewed weekly.

How targets get ranked

Size of the gap × account health × likelihood to buy. A healthy lab with three gaps goes first. A red lab gets fixed before anyone pitches. Tier 1 and 2 labs get a one-page account plan: current spend, gaps, next play, buyer, and owner.

📈
Run the plays in this order
Easiest first. Each play starts from evidence: a ticket pattern, a reorder drop, a change in the lab.
  1. 1ConsumablesMove labs buying blades and media elsewhere onto a supply program. Low risk and fast.
  2. 2Service plansOffer fuller coverage to labs with uncovered instruments or repeat repairs.
  3. 3TrainingTie it to a new surgeon or histotech, or to sectioning quality issues.
  4. 4CapitalStainer, embedding center, second cryostat, or replacement. Longer deals, so plan a year out.
Triggers that start a play
  • ›Repeat repairs on the same instrument
  • ›Reorders that slow down or stop
  • ›A new surgeon or histotech joins the lab
  • ›Stain quality complaints
  • ›An aging cryostat with rising repair cost
🚦
The health gate decides what gets sold
Scored in Power BI, green, yellow, or red.
Green
Full expansion plan. Consumables, service, training, and capital are all on the table.
Yellow
Sell carefully. Consumables and training only while the trend turns around.
Red
Fix first. The CSM calls within a week. No selling until it's fixed.
What goes into the score
  • ›Downtime and repeat tickets
  • ›Time to fix
  • ›Preventive maintenance done on schedule
  • ›Reorder trend
  • ›Contract end date
  • ›Training gaps or staff changes
👥
Who does what
The CSM finds the need. The KAM owns the big deals. The coordinators keep the labs running.
Customer success manager
Leads expansion in existing labs. Quotes consumables, service plans, and training. Owns account plans and the health view.
Key account manager
Owns capital deals, pricing, contracts, and new labs. Accepts or declines CS-sourced opportunities within a few days and logs why.
Service and support coordinator
Runs daily service: tickets, dispatch, parts, and loaners. Approves loaners and rush ships within set limits. Flags frustrated labs to the CSM.
Working rhythm
  • ›Daily: CSM and coordinators check open and aging tickets
  • ›Weekly: CSM and KAMs review at-risk accounts and open opportunities
  • ›Monthly: health score review and repeat issues
  • ›Quarterly: on-site reviews for top accounts
Territory and travel (proposed)
  • ›Florida gets about half of on-site visits
  • ›Group trips by area: never fly for one lab if you can see three
  • ›Georgia and Tennessee lean on video between visits
  • ›Add a second CSM when Florida fills the calendar
One rule
No pitch to a lab with an open problem. The CSM checks health before any conversation about growth.

The first 90 days
Days 1 to 30
Match accounts across both systems. Pull current spend per lab. Build the health score and tiers.
Days 31 to 60
Start daily and weekly rhythms. Visit Florida's top accounts. Write account plans.
Days 61 to 90
Launch the CS-sourced opportunity process. Run the first consumables and service plan plays. Fix the scoring.
Settle before launch
How CSM-sourced deals are credited in comp. What the CSM can quote alone. How far coordinators can go on loaners and rush ships. Who covers a down lab while the CSM travels. Who owns the account ID match.
