Growing inside existing Mohs labs

A customer success plan for a company that sells equipment, supplies, and service to Mohs surgery labs. One CSM. Three states. Most of the work is growing accounts the company already has.

Fix first, then grow
Proposed plan, not delivered work
I built this from public information about the company. The labs and account data below are made up to show how the method works. Percentages and plays are proposals.
The situation

The company sells cryostats, stainers, embedding equipment, consumables, service plans, and training to Mohs surgery labs. Most of the business is inside labs it already serves.

One CSM covers Florida, Georgia, and Tennessee. Florida is the biggest market.

The problem

Mohs surgery runs same day. A supply gap or a broken instrument delays patients. Selling a second product to a lab with an open problem costs trust.

The goal: grow revenue inside existing labs without hurting uptime.

The process
Six steps, run on repeat for every account.
STEP 1
Map accounts
Match sales and service records on one account ID. Pull current spend per lab.
STEP 2
Score health
Green, yellow, or red from downtime, repeat tickets, reorders, and contract date.
STEP 3
Find the gaps
White space map: what each lab buys from us, buys elsewhere, or doesn't have.
STEP 4
Rank and plan
Gap size, health, and likelihood to buy. One-page plan for top accounts.
STEP 5
Run the play
Consumables, then service plans, then training, then capital.
STEP 6
Hand off and track
CSM logs the opportunity. KAM accepts or declines. Reviewed weekly.

Run the plays in this order
Easiest first. Each play starts from evidence: a ticket pattern, a reorder drop, a change in the lab.
  1. 1ConsumablesMove labs buying blades and media elsewhere onto a supply program. Low risk and fast.
  2. 2Service plansOffer fuller coverage to labs with uncovered instruments or repeat repairs.
  3. 3TrainingTie it to a new surgeon or histotech, or to sectioning quality issues.
  4. 4CapitalStainer, embedding center, second cryostat, or replacement. Longer deals, so plan a year out.
The health gate decides what we sell
Scored in Power BI from downtime and repeat tickets, time to fix, preventive maintenance on schedule, reorder trend, contract end date, and training gaps.
Green

Full expansion plan. Consumables, service, training, and capital are all on the table.

Yellow

Sell carefully. Consumables and training only while the trend turns around.

Red

Fix first. The CSM calls within a week. No selling until it's fixed.

Who does what
The CSM finds the need. The KAM owns the big deals. The coordinators keep the labs running.
Customer success manager

Leads expansion in existing labs. Quotes consumables, service plans, and training. Owns account plans and the health view. Meets daily with coordinators and weekly with KAMs.

Key account manager

Owns capital deals, pricing, contracts, and new labs. Accepts or declines CS-sourced opportunities within a few days and logs why.

Service and support coordinator

Runs daily service: tickets, dispatch, parts, and loaners. Approves loaners and rush ships within set limits. Tells the CSM when a lab sounds frustrated.

Two systems, one account ID
Service data and sales data have to line up before anything else works.
Power BI

Service data. Tickets, uptime, preventive maintenance, repairs, and the health score.

One account ID
Dynamics

Sales data. Accounts, contracts, orders, and opportunities.

When the CSM spots a need, they log a CS-sourced opportunity in Dynamics with a source tag and a note on the trigger. The health score shows in Dynamics so KAMs see it. A link to the account shows in Power BI.

What to measure
  • Products per account
  • Consumables share of wallet
  • Service plan attach and renewal rate
  • Expansion revenue and pipeline created
  • Uptime and time to fix

Baselines in the first quarter. Targets after.

Days 1 to 30Match accounts across both systems. Pull current spend per lab. Build the health score and tiers.
Days 31 to 60Start daily and weekly rhythms. Visit Florida's top accounts. Write account plans.
Days 61 to 90Launch the CS-sourced opportunity process. Run the first consumables and service plan plays. Fix the scoring.
Settle these before launch
How CSM-sourced deals are credited in comp. What the CSM can quote alone. How far coordinators can go on loaners and rush ships. Who covers a down lab while the CSM travels. Who owns the account ID match.
